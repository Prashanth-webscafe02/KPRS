§ 02 — On method

A transformer is not assembled. It is wound — one turn of conductor at a time, under controlled tension, around a core cut to a hundredth of a millimetre. Every decision afterwards, from the oil to the radiators to the final test, exists to protect what was wound.

We design, wind, dry, tank and test under one roof. Nothing that carries current is subcontracted.

CRGO M4 core · Paper-insulated copper · Vapour-phase drying

§ 03 — Section through a 63 MVA unit

Anatomy of
a power transformer

  1. 03.1

    Tank & cooling

    A welded mild-steel tank, shot-blasted and epoxy-coated, carries the oil that insulates and cools. Detachable radiator banks shed the heat by natural convection, with fans for forced-air rating.

    Tank wall — 10 mm, stiffened
  2. 03.2

    Cover & bushings

    The cover carries the bushings that bring the windings out through the tank, the conservator that lets the oil breathe, and the protection — Buchholz, pressure relief, winding temperature.

    Cover assembly lifted
  3. 03.3

    Core

    Three limbs of cold-rolled grain-oriented steel, stacked in a step-lap pattern with mitred joints. The stepped section fills the circle; the step-lap keeps no-load loss and noise down.

    CRGO, step-lap, 7 steps
  4. 03.4

    Windings

    Low-voltage winding nearest the core, high-voltage outside, separated by pressboard barriers and oil ducts. Copper conductor, paper-insulated, clamped and pre-shrunk before it ever sees the tank.

    HV disc winding — Cu, PICC
  5. 03.5

    Reassembled

    Active part dried under vacuum, lowered into the tank, filled with degassed oil and held for impregnation. Then the test bay — and nothing ships without its record.

    Ready for routine test
